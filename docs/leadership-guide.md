# Eastern State Strategic Plan: leadership guide

Updated July 30, 2026
Audience: Eastern State leadership, Board reviewers, and first-time users

## The short version

This product gives Eastern State one place to follow the 2025–2029 Strategic
Plan. It brings together the five Strategic Priorities, 22 Strategic Goals, and
59 Measures that show whether the plan is moving forward.

The five Strategic Priorities are:

1. Reimagine Visitor Experience
2. Advance Historic Preservation
3. Expand Workforce Development
4. Support Learning through Justice Education
5. Enhance Organizational Capacity

The product is designed to answer four questions:

1. What is progressing?
2. What needs leadership attention?
3. What information or decisions are still missing?
4. What evidence supports the result we are discussing?

It does not turn missing information into a zero or quietly treat an unfinished
definition as a failure. If the product cannot support a conclusion, it says
so.

> **Important:** A fresh installation includes sample reporting values so the
> product can be reviewed before live information is entered. The product marks
> them with a **Sample data** badge. Do not present those values as approved
> Eastern State results. The Admin must confirm or replace them as part of
> launch.

## Why this exists

Strategic reporting becomes difficult when targets, source data, calculations,
notes, and presentation files live in different places. People can spend more
time reconciling versions than discussing the plan.

This product keeps the visible results, supporting details, and Board exports
connected to the same information. That gives leadership a clearer view of
progress and makes unresolved work visible before a review meeting.

## What you will see

The product has four main areas:

| Area | What it is for | Who can use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Overview** | A concise picture of the plan, the five Strategic Priorities, and the items that need attention | Board, Viewer, and Admin |
| **Reports** | The detailed Board Report, trends, and downloadable report formats | Board, Viewer, and Admin |
| **Data Entry** | A reporting checklist and focused forms for recording results | Admin |
| **Setup** | Strategic Plans, Measures, Goals, People, and a permanent history of changes | Admin |

You may also open a Strategic Priority or an individual Measure for more
detail. Those pages support the four destinations above; they are not separate systems
to learn.

## Who does what

The role names describe permissions, not seniority.

### Admin

An Admin can view everything, record results, manage Measures and Goals, manage
accounts, and review the history of changes.

For the initial installation, **Zach Palmer is the Admin**.

### Viewer

A Viewer can read the Overview, detailed results, and Reports. A Viewer cannot
change results, settings, or accounts.

For the initial installation, **Kerry Sautner is the Viewer**.

### Board

A Board account is read-only. It sees the Overview and Reports, limited to the
information that an Admin has approved for Board reporting.

## How to use the product

### First sign-in

Your system operator will give you the website address, your email address, and
a temporary password through a private channel.

1. Open the website and sign in with the temporary password.
2. The product will ask you to create a new password before showing any
   strategic information.
3. After you create the new password, you will be signed out.
4. Sign in again with your new password.

The second sign-in is intentional. It confirms that the temporary password and
the temporary session can no longer be used.

No one should ask you to find a password in a log, configuration file, or
email. If the temporary password does not work, contact the system operator.

### Preparing for a leadership or Board review

1. Open **Overview** and choose the Reporting Year.
2. Read the organization summary and the five Strategic Priorities.
3. Review **Needs attention** before treating the overall percentage as the
   complete story.
4. Open a Priority or Measure when you need the target, source, calculation,
   history, or explanation behind a result.
5. Open **Reports** for the detailed Board Report or trends.
6. Export only from the report currently shown on screen. The visible report
   and its export use the same underlying information.

### Completing a reporting cycle

This work requires the Admin role.

1. Open **Data Entry** and select the Reporting Year and period.
2. Use the checklist to see what is complete and what still needs attention.
3. Open one Measure and record the requested information.
4. Save and wait for the confirmed **Saved** message.
5. Continue until the expected Measures are complete or their unresolved
   issues have been clearly identified.

If a save fails, the product keeps the draft so the Admin can correct the issue
or try again. It does not report a save as complete before the server confirms
it.

### Continuing after 2029

The product does not stop working in 2029. An Admin can extend the current
plan's last reporting year in **Setup → Goals → Plan settings**. When the year
is extended, the new year appears automatically in Overview, Data Entry,
Reports, and the detailed result pages.

Extending the years does not automatically invent future targets or assume
that every Measure should keep the same definition. After the extension, the
Admin should review each Measure, create any needed future version of its
setup, add targets for the new year, and confirm what the Board may see. This
keeps past reports from changing when a Measure is updated.

There is one important boundary: this workflow extends the current Strategic
Plan. The product does not yet provide a normal screen for creating a separate
successor plan, preparing it alongside the current plan, and then activating
it. If Eastern State plans to adopt a distinct plan after 2029 rather than
extend the current one, schedule that transition with the system maintainer
before the new plan begins.

## How to read the information

### Short forms used in the approved plan

Some Measure names come directly from Eastern State's plan and supporting
workbooks. In those names:

- **ESPHS** means Eastern State Penitentiary Historic Site.
- **Year over year** means compared with the previous year. A source Measure
  may shorten this to **YOY**.
- **PD workshop** means a professional-development workshop.
- **PWYW event** means a pay-what-you-wish event.
- **NPS** means Net Promoter Score, a survey score used to summarize how likely
  someone is to recommend an experience.

These are plan terms, not software instructions. An Admin can make a Measure's
name or explanation clearer in Setup without changing its recorded results.

### “Needs attention” is not the same as poor performance

An item may need attention because it has no finalized target, no complete
measurement definition, a missing owner, or another unresolved decision. Open
the item to see the reason.

### Missing is not zero

No reported value means “we do not yet have a usable result.” It does not mean
the result was zero.

### A target and a Strategic Goal are different

A **Strategic Goal** is an outcome Eastern State wants to achieve. A **Target**
is the expected result for a particular Measure. One Goal may depend on several
Measures.

### The percentage needs its denominator

When the product shows completion, it also shows the underlying count—for
example, “7 of 12 Goals completed.” Items that cannot be evaluated remain
visible with a reason instead of being silently counted as failures.

### Board Status is a management assessment

Board Status is a stated assessment such as on track or at risk. It is not a
replacement for the calculated result, target progress, or readiness of the
underlying information.

## What leadership should confirm before launch

The software is ready to support the reporting process, but leadership still
owns the reporting decisions around it. Before announcing the product as the
official source, confirm:

- the five Strategic Priorities, 22 Strategic Goals, and 59 Measures reflect
  the approved plan;
- each unresolved target or measurement definition has an owner and a decision
  date;
- sample reporting values have been confirmed or replaced before anyone treats
  the product as the official record;
- the Reporting Year and review schedule are agreed;
- leadership has decided whether the years after 2029 extend the current plan
  or belong to a distinct successor plan;
- people know who requests, checks, enters, and approves each result;
- Zach should hold the Admin role and Kerry should hold the Viewer role;
- the Board visibility selection contains only the information approved for
  Board use;
- one named system operator owns backups, updates, account recovery, and
  service checks; and
- the old spreadsheet or report process will either be retired or clearly
  identified as an archive, so two competing sources do not develop.

## What this product protects

- People must sign in, and changing a password or role ends older sessions.
- Only an Admin can change strategic information or accounts.
- The history records who changed important information and when.
- Missing or invalid information remains visible instead of being rewritten as
  a favorable result.
- The health check used by the server reveals no accounts, plan details, file
  locations, or secrets.
- The database must be backed up before migration or release.

## Questions a leader can ask during the first walkthrough

- Can I tell which Strategic Priorities need discussion in less than two
  minutes?
- Can I open a result and understand its target, source, date, and caveats?
- Does “Needs attention” tell me who should resolve the issue?
- Does the Board Report match what I see on screen?
- Are any Measures being tracked elsewhere because this workflow does not yet
  meet the team's needs?
- Who will confirm that a reporting cycle is ready for review?

Answers to those questions are more useful than a tour of the software's
technical architecture.

## If something goes wrong

- **Cannot sign in or forgot a password:** contact the system operator or an
  Admin.
- **Wrong role or missing access:** ask an Admin to review Setup → People.
- **A result looks wrong:** open the Measure and check its Reporting Year,
  target, source, inputs, and notes before changing anything.
- **The website is unavailable:** contact the system operator. Do not try to
  repair the server from this guide.
- **A definition or target is disputed:** leave it identified as unresolved
  until the responsible leader decides it; do not enter zero as a placeholder.
